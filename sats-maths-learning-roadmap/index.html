A PARENT'S GUIDE TO THE YEAR 6 SATs

Year 6 SATs Maths: what parents need to know

Year 6 SATs assess how securely children understand and apply the mathematics taught during primary school.

The results help parents and teachers recognise strengths, identify areas where further support may be useful, and provide information for the transition to secondary school.

They are an important educational checkpoint, not a complete measure of a child's mathematical ability.

THE THREE MATHS PAPERS

How the Maths tests are structured

PAPER 1

Arithmetic

30 minutes | 40 marks

Tests calculation fluency and accuracy, including arithmetic with whole numbers, fractions and decimals.

PAPER 2

Reasoning

40 minutes | 35 marks

Assesses how pupils apply their mathematical knowledge to solve problems and interpret information.

PAPER 3

Reasoning

40 minutes | 35 marks

A second reasoning assessment containing further mathematical problems and questions requiring pupils to choose and apply appropriate methods.

The three papers carry 110 marks altogether. Calculators are not permitted in the standard Maths tests.

2027 dates: Wednesday 12 May — Maths Papers 1 and 2; Thursday 13 May — Maths Paper 3. Timings, marks and equipment rules follow the 2027 parent information and should be read alongside the latest official guidance.

UNDERSTANDING SCALED SCORES

What does your child's SATs Maths score mean?

80–99Below the expected standard
100–109Expected standard achieved
110–120Above the threshold, not a separate reported standard

The marks from all three Maths papers are combined into a raw score out of 110. This is converted into a scaled score, normally ranging from 80 to 120.

A scaled score of 100 represents the expected standard for the end of Key Stage 2. It does not mean that a child answered every question correctly.

The raw marks needed for a particular scaled score can change each year because test difficulty can vary. Pupils with very low raw marks may not receive a numerical scaled score.

WHY RESULTS MATTER

A national picture alongside wider school information

SATs provide a nationally standardised indication of attainment at the end of primary school.

Parents receive results through their child's school, alongside wider information about achievements and progress. Teachers and secondary schools can use the information to identify strengths and areas where pupils may benefit from further teaching or support.

The results also contribute to national reporting on primary school performance.

THE MOVE TO YEAR 7

Can SATs results affect secondary school Maths sets?

Secondary schools receive Key Stage 2 assessment information when children transfer from primary school.

Where schools use Maths sets, SATs results may contribute to decisions about initial teaching groups. However, schools may also consider teacher information, classroom performance and their own assessments.

There is no single national SATs score that automatically determines a child's Year 7 Maths set. A child's initial teaching group should not be presented as a permanent judgement of their potential.

A useful question for your child's secondary school

“How do you decide initial Year 7 Maths groups, when are pupils reassessed, and how can they move between groups?”

A GUIDE FOR PARENTS

What does secure understanding look like?

A child may answer a routine question correctly yet struggle to choose the same method in a new situation. Teachers learn more by looking at explanations, calculations, common errors and how well learning transfers to other questions.

How to use this page

Explore one strand, ask your child what feels familiar, look at an illustrated example together, and discuss uncertainties with their teacher. Do not use this webpage to diagnose your child's attainment independently.
