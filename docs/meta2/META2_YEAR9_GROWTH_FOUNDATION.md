# Meta 2.0 Year 9 Maths Growth Foundation

Status: Current launch-state record as of 05 October 2026. Strategy and metric guidance below remains subject to its stated decision boundaries; historical review figures are retained as historical evidence.

## 1. Purpose

Meta 2.0 exists to produce **REGISTERED, CONTINUING YEAR 9 MATHS STUDENTS AT SUSTAINABLE CAC**. Maximum clicks and maximum leads are not the objective. CTR, CPC and CPL help diagnose the funnel; even a paid £100 starter is an intermediate result. Continuation, retained revenue, delivery capacity and acquisition economics determine whether growth is sustainable.

This document uses the current Year 9 public entry route in `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` and `year-9-maths.html` and records the final authorised Meta 2.0 publication state supplied on 05 October 2026. The earlier draft and 02 October commercial-review assumptions are historical, not current campaign or commercial policy. Unapproved scaling thresholds and metric definitions remain proposals where labelled.

## 2. Why Meta 2.0 Exists

The reviewed Meta period, approximately 24 September–1 October 2026, produced about **£210.54 spend, 14 raw leads, 0 assessments, 0 consultations and 0 admissions**. That did not prove Meta incapable of producing useful demand. It showed that raw demand did not become meaningful downstream outcomes in that test. The cause was not isolated: year groups, form versions, lead maturity and follow-up varied. Meta 2.0 therefore measures the whole route from attention to continuing student, with each handoff owned and checked.

## 3. Growth Model

| Stage | Primary influence | What the stage must establish |
|---|---|---|
| Spend → impressions → outbound clicks | Meta delivery and creative | The right Year 9 parents notice a relevant promise and choose to learn more. |
| Clicks → landing-page views → enquiries | Website, message match and form | The page loads, explains the offer and records an informed enquiry. |
| Enquiries → reachable parents → qualified enquiries | Admissions and follow-up | A real parent can be contacted, understands the offer and appears suitable. |
| Qualified → consultations booked → attended | Admissions and scheduling | The parent understands the next step and a conversation actually happens. |
| Consultation → free diagnostic | Admissions and academic team | The free initial diagnostic is arranged and completed before a paid starter. |
| Diagnostic → suitable group match | Teaching, timetable and capacity | A compatible group, place and lesson dates exist before payment is requested. |
| Match → prepaid £100 starter | Admissions and payment process | Terms are clear and payment is received for four complete one-hour lessons. |
| Starter → four-lesson completion → continuation → retained revenue | Teaching, parent experience and finance | Progress and group fit are reviewed; any continuation is explicitly agreed. |

Measure each transition. Broken follow-up, no capacity, poor scheduling, group mismatch or weak teaching must not be diagnosed as a Meta delivery failure. Equally, weak or misleading creative can attract the wrong parents even when the page works.

The current public offer is **Year 9 Maths only**: a free initial diagnostic; online small-group teaching at **£25/hour** with **four students**; a **£100 prepaid starter for four complete one-hour lessons** after suitability, a place and lesson dates are confirmed; review of progress and fit after those lessons; then £25/hour continuation or an explicitly agreed fixed monthly arrangement. There is no automatic monthly conversion or renewal into a monthly arrangement. Unused prepaid teaching is refunded if the family stops. AQA, Edexcel and OCR are supported. Places depend on a suitable group match. The £100 starter is not a promise of a place or a long-term commitment.

| Level | Metric | Decision role |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term north star | **Media cost per retained/continuing Year 9 student**, alongside collected and retained revenue and ultimately fully loaded CAC | Shows whether acquisition becomes sustainable teaching revenue. The retention horizon is **UNRESOLVED — PRAKASH DECISION REQUIRED**. |
| Near-term commercial proxy | **Media CAC per paid £100 starter** | Earlier indicator while the four lessons and continuation have not matured. |

| Top-funnel diagnostics | Spend; impressions; CPM; outbound clicks; outbound CTR; outbound CPC; LPVs; click-to-LPV rate; enquiries; CPL | Locate delivery or page friction, never choose a winner on their own. |

## 5. Creative Philosophy

Creative influences who stops, what problem they recognise, what expectation they form and whether they investigate. The three current strategic concepts are:

| Concept | Parent thought | Core angle |
|---|---|---|
| **A — GCSE Foundations** | “I don't want Maths gaps becoming a GCSE problem.” | Strengthen Year 9 foundations before gaps compound. |
| **B — Maths Confidence** | “My child is capable, but confidence in Maths is slipping.” | Restore confidence through structured, supportive Maths teaching. |
| **C — Parent Proof** | “I want evidence that this teaching actually helps families like ours.” | Show specific, permissioned evidence of teaching and progress. |

These are distinct parent problems and reasons to act. Colour changes, small headline edits and minor layout changes are variants within a concept. Test concepts through their downstream cohorts, not just their delivery statistics.

## 6. Creative Winner Definition

A winning creative is not automatically the one with the highest CTR, lowest CPC or cheapest CPL. It combines enough attention and deliberate page traffic with genuine enquiries, qualified parents, attended consultations, paid starters, continuing students and acceptable CAC.

For example, Creative X can have lower CTR and higher CPC than Creative Y yet be commercially stronger if a larger share of its parents qualify, attend and pay. Compare cohorts at similar maturity and record spend by creative. Where there is insufficient volume or attribution, report **INSUFFICIENT DATA** rather than inventing a winner. There are no assumed Meta CTR or CPC benchmarks in this plan.

## 7. Offer and Message Boundary

> **THE AD'S JOB:** Attract the right Year 9 parent strongly enough to want more information. Lead with a parent pain or concern, desired child outcome, emotional relevance and a credible reason to investigate.
>
> **THE LANDING PAGE'S JOB:** Convert and qualify that interest by explaining teaching structure, group size, price, starter arrangement, teachers, proof, diagnostic, joining journey and next step.

Group size and fee can support an ad's credibility, but they need not dominate a cold-audience concept. The page must explain them clearly before enquiry. Do not make the ad carry every programme detail, and do not hide a material term on the page. The current Year 9 page invites a consultation and explains that the free diagnostic and group match precede payment.

## 8. Capacity as a Scaling Constraint

Meta cannot scale beyond Jothi's ability to place suitable students. Record **qualified demand with no suitable group** separately from an unqualified enquiry. Review compatible group availability, teacher capacity, timetable, level and pace, actual places and follow-up capacity before adding spend. Aggregate “spaces” do not prove a particular child can be placed.

## 9. Improvement Loop

```text
MEASURE → locate the largest meaningful funnel leak → form a hypothesis
→ change one important variable → record the change → gather new evidence
→ compare equivalent, mature cohorts → keep / revert / iterate
```

Record the date, prior state, change and expected effect. Avoid changing creative, landing page, offer and follow-up process together, except to repair a genuine broken route. A technical failure takes priority over a clean experiment. Compare against the actual business outcome after enough time for parents to progress.

## 10. Scaling Philosophy

High CTR, cheap CPC and cheap leads are insufficient reasons to scale. Before meaningful budget expansion, require evidence of genuine Year 9 demand, lead quality, working consultation and admissions follow-up, paid starters, early continuation, compatible group capacity, acceptable CAC and sound measurement.

The previous independent review suggested approximately **8–10 paid admissions across more than one cohort** before assuming repeatability. **REFERENCE GUIDANCE — NOT A HARD STATISTICAL RULE.** This does not approve a budget increase or define a required sample size. The exact scaling threshold and acceptable CAC are **UNRESOLVED — PRAKASH DECISION REQUIRED**.

## 11. Failure Diagnosis Matrix

| Symptom | Likely stage | Questions to ask | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| High CPM | Auction/delivery | Is the audience, placement or creative reaching the intended parent? Is spend sufficient to judge? | That the offer failed. |
| Low outbound CTR | Creative attention/relevance | Is the parent concern clear? Are there enough impressions for a comparison? | That cheaper clicks would be better parents. |
| Good CTR, poor landing-page-view rate | Click → page | Is mobile load, link, consent or tracking working? Are clicks accidental? | That the page copy alone is weak. |
| Good page views, poor enquiry rate | Page/offer/form | Is the Year 9 offer, price, proof and next step clear? Does the form work? | That Meta supplied low-quality traffic. |
| Many enquiries, low reachability | Form/follow-up | Are details valid? Is contact prompt and expected? Is the owner clear? | That every unusual profile is fraudulent. |
| Reachable, low qualification | Creative/page targeting and expectation | Were year group, subject, group format and starter terms understood? | That CTR proves good demand. |
| Qualified, no consultation | Scheduling/admissions | Can parents book promptly? Are reminders and ownership working? | That the ad failed. |
| Consultations, no diagnostics | Admissions/academic handoff | Was a diagnostic appropriate, offered and scheduled? | That all consultations were poor leads. |
| Diagnostics, no suitable group | Capacity/matching | Are level, pace, timetable, teacher and places compatible? | That marketing generated unqualified demand. |
| Suitable group, no payment | Terms/payment/admissions | Were dates, £100 starter, refunds and next step clear? | That acquisition alone caused the loss. |
| Starters, poor continuation | Teaching/product/fit | Were lessons delivered, progress visible and group fit reviewed? | That a cheap starter CAC means growth works. |

## 12. Governance

- No fake scarcity or exaggerated grade claims. Proof must be accurate and permissioned.
- No unnecessary parent or student personal data, diagnostic content or unrestricted CRM notes in advertising measurement.
- Do not manufacture duplicate conversions. Reconcile Meta events against recorded enquiries and preserve source evidence.
- Marketing measurement requires applicable consent; service/contact processing and advertising consent are distinct.
- Do not scale where fulfilment capacity is absent.
- Keep the original acquisition source when a parent later uses email, WhatsApp or telephone.

### Attribution contract and current implementation boundary

Every Meta 2.0 destination URL **MUST** contain `utm_source=meta`. The supplied URL standard is `utm_source=meta&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=meta2_y9_maths_oct26&utm_content=<creative_identifier>`.

In `year-9-maths.js`, `utm_source=meta` classifies the enquiry marker as `meta`; `utm_source=openai` classifies it as `openai`; absent or other values become `unknown`. The form writes `marketing_platform=Meta` and the currently listed UTMs into Bigin Description for Meta traffic, and leaves `POTENTIALCF12` blank. For OpenAI traffic it writes `POTENTIALCF12=OpenAI Ads`; `year-9-maths-success.js` gates OpenAI `lead_created` on `marker.source=openai`. Unknown traffic fails closed for platform classification. Meta traffic cannot fire the OpenAI `lead_created` path. Do not infer a Meta or OpenAI source without explicit evidence.

The local form attribution list now includes `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content` and `oppref`. A supplied `utm_content` is added to the existing Bigin Description payload using the same 200-character, whitespace-normalising sanitisation as the other UTMs. Use non-personal creative identifiers; do not put parent or student information in destination UTMs. A controlled Bigin record verified receipt of Meta attribution and `utm_content=foundations_static`; first genuine lead receipt and downstream record linkage remain to verify. Meta campaign/ad/ad-set IDs are not separately captured. `script.js` sends a consent-gated Meta browser `Lead` on the Year 9 success route from its own pending-enquiry marker; the reviewed code does not source-gate that Meta event. Do not equate a Meta-reported Lead with a verified Bigin Meta enquiry.
